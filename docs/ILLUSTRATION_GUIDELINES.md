# Illustration system

**Art direction:** Expressive flat 2D illustration for Indian neighbourhood commerce — confident
cut-paper shapes, stylized local characters, warm colors, subtle grain, and generous whitespace.
Use angular faces and flowing hair while keeping the scene simplified and UI-friendly.

Use this family across MithraDirect. Illustrations support the interface; keep the task, content,
and primary action visually dominant. Use light or white backgrounds, generous whitespace,
broad shapes, selective fine contour lines, and flat color areas. Sparse subtle speckle grain may
add warmth inside the artwork; keep the surrounding background quiet. Avoid lighting gradients,
modeled shading, material rendering, and glossy highlights. Raster assets must retain the same
flat graphic appearance.

Use solid color fills rather than painted lighting or material shading. Simplify hair, clothing,
flowers, bread, and produce into clear silhouettes with only the marks needed for recognition.
Faces use expressive almond eyes, graphic brows, elongated angular noses, and a few marks for
the mouth. Hair is a flowing dark silhouette with a few fine curls. Use gently exaggerated
proportions and readable gestures; keep expressions warm and engaged with local commerce.
Avoid generic dot-eye cartoon faces and realistic anatomical detail.

Emerald is the primary anchor. Warm complementary illustration colors include mustard,
terracotta, coral, cream, muted teal, and soft yellow. These colors belong inside the artwork;
application controls continue to use the semantic UI palette.

Characters are friendly, expressive local shopkeepers, customers, and delivery partners in an
Indian neighbourhood. Use recognizable kirana stores, produce stalls, cafés, pharmacies, bakeries,
and other local shops, with enough detail to communicate the activity clearly.

Use restrained everyday Indian details: sari borders, jasmine in tied hair, small jhumka earrings,
kurta collars, dupattas, cotton checks, and a folded gamcha where appropriate. Local products such
as pav can identify the shops. Keep these cues contemporary and simple; avoid elaborate costumes,
landmarks, or festival decoration unrelated to the depicted activity.

Marketplace artwork must connect neighbourhood commerce to online ordering. Use a recognizable
digital storefront with product thumbnails and a shopping basket, a customer browsing on a phone,
or a shopkeeper receiving an order on their phone. Tie digital cues to the people and their local
shops. A market scene alone does not communicate MithraDirect's online marketplace. Keep the
digital storefront simple and pictorial, legible at the displayed size, and secondary to the app's
real controls; avoid generic dashboards and unrelated interface decoration.

Prefer phones held naturally by the characters over a large standalone phone preview. Keep
digital cues small and purposeful. Limit products, foliage, and decorative symbols to what identifies
the shops; leave breathing room between characters and stalls. People and their neighbourhood
remain the focus.

Show ordering through natural gestures: a customer taps their phone while a shopkeeper checks
theirs. Keep the digital story subtle; avoid connecting the phones with arrows or dotted paths.
The login panel pairs a heading with the illustration. Keep the scene free of floating order
badges or notification graphics.

Match existing artwork's character proportions, shapes, palette, contour weight, and subtle grain when
adding another illustration. Do not invent a different visual style per page.

The shared `/login` and `/vendor/login` reference is
[`neighbourhood-market-reference.png`](../public/images/auth/neighbourhood-market-reference.png):
three local shops, four stylized characters, and natural interactions with small handheld phones.

Suggested subjects:

- Vendor onboarding: a shopkeeper setting up a digital storefront.
- Empty catalog: local shelves waiting for products.
- New order: a shopkeeper receiving an order notification.
- Delivery: a local delivery partner carrying an order to a customer.
- Store approved: a vendor opening their shop with a welcoming community feel.
- Subscription or billing: a storefront with a simple calendar or payment motif.
- Success: a thriving local shop with customers.

Avoid generic corporate SaaS illustrations, oversized dashboards or abstract UI blocks beside
people, charts and gears, heavy gradients, glossy 3D characters, futuristic or neon visuals,
dark backgrounds, stock clipart, and excessively detailed or realistic scenes. Avoid semi-realistic
figures and painterly rendering, even when described as vector art.
